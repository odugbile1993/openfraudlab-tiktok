## In a nutshell

Data science turns raw data into insights and better decisions by combining statistics, programming and domain knowledge.

## Key ideas

- **Definition:** Turning raw data into insights and better decisions.
- **3 ingredients:** Statistics / Programming / Domain knowledge — Reason about data · work with it at scale · know which questions matter
- **The workflow:** Question → Collect → Clean → Explore → Model → Communicate
- **Beginner surprise:** Cleaning data is often the most time-consuming part.
- **Real example:** "Which customers are likely to repay?" — Past repayment records → clean → find patterns → model → better lending decisions
- **Takeaway:** Data science isn't just models. / It's asking good questions of data.

## Try it yourself

Pick a decision you make often (for example, what to buy, or when to leave for work). Write down one question data could help you answer, and list the data you would need to collect.

Welcome to Data Science from Scratch. Lesson one: what is data science, really?

Data science is the practice of turning raw data into useful insights, and better decisions.

It sits where three things meet. Statistics, to reason about data. Programming, to work with it at scale. And domain knowledge, to know which questions actually matter.

Most projects follow the same loop. Ask a question. Collect the data. Clean it. Explore it. Build a model if you need one. Then communicate what you found.

And here's what surprises most beginners: cleaning and preparing the data is often the most time-consuming part of the job.

For example, a lender might ask: which customers are likely to repay? A data scientist gathers past repayment records, cleans them, finds patterns, and builds a model to support that decision.

So remember: data science isn't just about models. It's about asking good questions of data.
