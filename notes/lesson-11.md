## In a nutshell

Choose a chart by the question you are answering: bar charts compare categories, line charts show trends over time, histograms and box plots show distributions, and scatter plots show the relationship between two numeric columns.

## Key ideas

- **Start here:** What question are you answering? — Compare · trend · distribution · relationship
- **Compare:** Bar chart — Transactions by payment method
- **Trend:** Line chart — Monthly loan applications
- **Distribution:** Histogram or box plot — How transaction amounts are spread out
- **Relationship:** Scatter plot — Income vs loan amount · one dot per customer
- **Takeaway:** Question first, then pick the chart.

## Try it yourself

Write four questions about your own spending, for example 'Which category do I spend most on?' or 'Is my spending rising month by month?'. For each one, name the chart you would use (bar, line, histogram or scatter) and sketch it on paper.

Data Science from Scratch, lesson eleven: choosing the right chart.

Start with the question, not the chart. Are you comparing groups, showing a trend over time, showing a distribution, or showing a relationship between two things?

To compare categories, use a bar chart, like the number of transactions by payment method. It's usually easier to read than a pie chart, especially with many slices. And start the bars at zero, so differences aren't exaggerated.

To show a trend over time, use a line chart, like monthly loan applications. Time usually goes along the horizontal axis, from left to right.

To show a distribution, use a histogram or a box plot. They show where most values sit, how spread out they are, and whether there are outliers.

To show the relationship between two number columns, use a scatter plot, like income against loan amount. Each dot is one customer.

So the takeaway: decide the question first, then pick the simplest chart that answers it.
